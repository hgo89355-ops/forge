# Owner taste — read before animating, staging or writing any video

Collected from the owner's feedback on the lock Short (v6 → v19, Nov 2026). These are standing preferences: apply
them by default so the owner doesn't have to repeat them. Newest notes win if anything conflicts.

## Overall look
- **Not cartoonish / not childish.** Grown-up, dry humour. No cute bouncing, no baby faces.
- **2D for story scenes** (Rick's-Clips-like flat style, customised to our guy), 3D only for the machine explanation.
  Realistic 3D characters were "awful"; boxy 3D stickmen looked like "a bot".
- **Night, moody lighting** for story scenes when it fits: dark blue night, warm practical light (porch lamp, lit
  window), falloff into shadow. Bright daytime flat colours read as a kids' cartoon.
- **Every scene is a real place** (never a void): house front, porch, bush, window, lamp.

## Camera
- **Low and close.** Near eye/head height, close on the character. Not high, not far, no bird's-eye.
- The viewer must **see where he stands** (floor/porch under his feet). No narrow strip "sidewalk", no walkway
  running off sideways that makes no sense.
- **Crop the set**: you don't need the whole door — a cropped door panel that reads like a wall is fine; the audience
  fills in the rest.
- Start between the important things (e.g. between the bush and the door), then move in on the character.

## The character's acting
- **Personality first** (docs/CHARACTER.md): overconfident, sneaky, deadpan. He must never look like a toy that barely
  moves.
- **Fully flexible body**: bendy rubber-hose limbs, hands that really move. Never "boxed" or robotic.
- **Proportions**: a bit tall, not fat, legs thick enough (not twig legs), white arms, bigger eyes / slightly bigger face
  than the first designs, mature (not "fat kid").
- **Sneaking must LOOK like sneaking**: low crouch, bent knees, tiptoes, hunched shoulders, hands up, head low —
  not normal walking. Quick little steps in bursts, then freeze and look.
- **Looking around must carry emotion** (nervous, suspicious, wide eyes on a sound) — not blank side-to-side head turns,
  and not constantly.
- **Struggles are real and physical**: key in → it won't turn → eyes widen → he fights it with his WHOLE BODY
  (both hands, leaning back, legs braced, shaking with effort) — real effort, not slapstick (no star eyes, no foot
  kick gags). Clenched teeth only at the peak of a max-effort pull (owner asked for more emotion there).
- **Expressions**: small, mature eyes; narrowed lids for effort; small mouths. Avoid star eyes, bug eyes, huge grins.
- Wins are cool and understated (smirk + small fist pump), not jumping around.

## Story & pacing
- **Explanation at 1.12× speed** (owner 2026-11-28, for retention; 1.05× was not noticeable): speed the part after the
  hook (atempo keeps the voice pitch). The hook itself stays at normal speed.
- Max-effort moments need a clear effort face (squeezed eyes, hard brows, clenched teeth, head jerking) — 'maxEffort'.
- **Hook = a story in the first second** ("A burglar is sneaking into your house in the middle of the night…"),
  second person ("you / your house"). Stakes first, then the "why".
- Something new every 2–3 s; camera always moving; cuts on words.
- When the voice must wait, the action must still be interesting (no dead air).

## Process
- **Before any long re-render after a style change, send ONE still frame** and wait for ✓.
- Re-render only the changed parts (splice), never the whole video when avoidable.
- Report in plain words; ask only for real decisions.
